## A calmer Kyoto strategy

Do not cross the city repeatedly. Give each day one district, start the famous places early, and let the rest of the day narrow into lanes, gardens, and tea.

## Day 1 — Higashiyama on foot

- Reach Kiyomizu-dera near opening time.
- Walk downhill through Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka before the busiest period.
- Continue to Kodai-ji and Maruyama Park, then enter Gion from the east.
- Spend the evening along the Kamo River or in Ponto-chō.

## Day 2 — Northern gardens

- Begin at Ginkaku-ji and follow the Philosopher's Path.
- Choose one or two smaller temples rather than every gate along the way.
- End near Nanzen-ji and the aqueduct; return by subway from Keage.

## Day 3 — Arashiyama beyond the bamboo

- Arrive early for the bamboo grove, then continue instead of turning back.
- Visit Tenryū-ji's garden and cross Togetsukyō Bridge.
- Reserve the afternoon for a quieter temple such as Jōjakkō-ji or a riverside walk.

## Day 4 — Fushimi and central Kyoto

- Walk the lower Fushimi Inari trail early; climb farther only if the weather and energy suit you.
- Return for Nishiki Market outside its busiest hour.
- Finish at Kyoto Station's roof garden or a neighbourhood sentō.

## Stay, eat, move

- **Stay:** Downtown near Karasuma/Oike for useful subway access; Higashiyama for atmosphere.
- **Eat:** Make one reservation, then leave the other meals flexible for soba, tofu, cafés, and market snacks.
- **Move:** Trains and subways are usually more predictable than buses; walk neighbouring sights together.

> Kyoto's official travel site publishes live congestion and transport guidance. Check it while planning each day.
